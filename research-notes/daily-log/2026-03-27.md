## Experiment Log

### EXP-005: Explanation Faithfulness Analysis

- **Time**: 2026-03-27
- **Objective**: Measure whether SHAP explanations are faithful representations of model behavior, and whether faithfulness degrades under perturbation
- **Configuration**: 3 datasets × 2 models × 5 seeds × 10 conditions = 300 records
- **Runtime**: 76.2 seconds
- **Output**: faithfulness_results.csv (300 rows)
- **Status**: Complete

#### Key Findings

**1. Surrogate fidelity collapses under label flip — can go NEGATIVE:**
- Diabetes + LogReg: baseline fidelity = 0.18 → label_flip@15%: **-0.50**
  - Negative fidelity = SHAP surrogate is WORSE than predicting the mean
  - SHAP is not just unstable, it becomes actively misleading
- Heart + LogReg: 0.63 → 0.12 (81% fidelity loss)
- Breast Cancer + LogReg: 0.94 → 0.34 (64% fidelity loss)

**2. Gaussian noise barely touches fidelity:**
- All fidelity values stay within ±0.01 of baseline
- SHAP remains a faithful model surrogate even under moderate input noise

**3. XGBoost maintains higher fidelity than LogReg under label flip:**
- Breast Cancer: XGB fidelity = 0.72 vs LR = 0.34 at 15% label flip
- Heart: XGB = 0.52 vs LR = 0.12
- XGBoost's TreeExplainer produces exact SHAP; LogReg's LinearExplainer assumes linearity which breaks when labels are corrupted

**4. Perturbation faithfulness ratio confirms SHAP correctness:**
- XGBoost FR_k3 = 100–400 (masking top-3 features causes 100–400x more prediction drop than bottom-3)
- LogReg FR_k3 = 17–120 (still faithful, but less discriminating)
- Ratios DECREASE under label flip (XGBoost: 238 → 11 for Breast Cancer) — SHAP's ability to identify the right features degrades as model quality degrades

### EXP-006: Statistical Significance Testing

- **Time**: 2026-03-27
- **Objective**: Determine whether observed stability differences are statistically and practically significant
- **Tests Conducted**: 51 tests total
- **Output**: statistical_tests.csv (51 rows), confidence_intervals.csv (216 rows)
- **Status**: Complete

#### Key Findings

**1. Label flip vs. other perturbations: ALL 6 comparisons p<0.001:**

| Dataset | Model | Label Flip ρ | Others ρ | Cohen's d |
|---------|-------|-------------|----------|-----------|
| Heart | XGBoost | 0.817 | 0.974 | 3.02 |
| Heart | LogReg | 0.745 | 0.994 | 3.34 |
| Diabetes | XGBoost | 0.919 | 0.979 | 1.73 |
| Diabetes | LogReg | 0.906 | 0.999 | 2.02 |
| Breast Cancer | XGBoost | 0.472 | 0.998 | **6.68** |
| Breast Cancer | LogReg | 0.281 | 0.996 | **8.70** |

- Cohen's d up to 8.70 — massive effect size (>0.8 = large)
- This is the strongest statistical finding in the study

**2. XGBoost vs LogReg: large effect sizes but p>0.05 (power issue):**
- Wilcoxon with n=5 seeds lacks power — p=0.0625 is the minimum possible with 5 pairs
- Cohen's d = 2.81 for Breast Cancer label_flip@5% — practically huge
- Recommendation for paper: report effect sizes alongside p-values; acknowledge power limitation

**3. Faithfulness degradation under label flip: 6/6 significant at p<0.01:**
- Confirmed that label flip degrades not just stability but actual explanation quality
- Gaussian noise: 5/6 NOT significant — fidelity is preserved
- Feature dropout: borderline (2/6 significant)

**4. Bootstrap CIs show clean separation for label flip conditions:**
- Label flip CIs do not overlap with Gaussian/dropout CIs in any dataset
- Breast Cancer label_flip CIs: ρ ∈ [0.15, 0.45] — entirely below 0.5

## Notes

- The negative fidelity finding (Diabetes + LogReg under label flip) is particularly publishable — it shows SHAP can become actively misleading, not just noisy.
- The power limitation (n=5 seeds) is inherent to the 5-day timeline. Paper should recommend n≥20 seeds for definitive paired comparisons.
- Total experiment count across all 3 days: 300 (Day 1) + 4590 (Day 2) + 300 (Day 3) + 51 (Day 3 tests) + 216 (Day 3 CIs) = **5,457 data points** from a 5-day study.
