### EXP-003: Per-Feature Stability Analysis + Model-Space Lipschitz

- **Time**: 2026-03-26
- **Objective**: Track how each individual feature's SHAP attribution shifts under perturbation; introduce model-space Lipschitz metric for label-flip (fixes Day 1 gap where input-space Lipschitz = 0)
- **Configuration**: Same 3 datasets × 2 models × 5 seeds × 9 perturbation configs = 4590 per-feature records + 90 model-Lipschitz records
- **Runtime**: 25.0 seconds
- **Output**: per_feature_stability.csv (4590 rows), model_lipschitz.csv (90 rows)
- **Status**: Complete

#### Key Findings

**1. Most unstable features are the most discriminative:**
- Heart Disease: `ca` (coronary vessels) and `thal` (thalassemia type) are most unstable — both are the strongest clinical predictors
- Diabetes: `Glucose` is overwhelmingly the most unstable (ΔSHAP = 0.63) — also the dominant predictor
- Breast Cancer: `worst area` (ΔSHAP = 1.24) and `worst concave points` — key tumor morphology features
- Pattern: features with the highest baseline importance shift the most under label corruption

**2. Model-space Lipschitz reveals 10–20x asymmetry between model families:**
- XGBoost: L_θ = 8.5–15.6 (massive SHAP sensitivity per unit of parameter change)
- LogReg: L_θ = 0.72–0.87 (nearly constant, regardless of dataset)
- Interpretation: tree ensembles amplify small parameter changes into large explanation shifts; linear models dampen them
- This is the Day 1 gap resolved — label flip now has a proper sensitivity metric

**3. Model-space Lipschitz decreases with perturbation intensity (XGBoost):**
- Heart: L_θ = 15.6 (5%) → 10.2 (10%) → 9.6 (15%)
- Diabetes: L_θ = 13.5 (5%) → 10.5 (10%) → 10.9 (15%)
- Small label corruptions are proportionally more disruptive than large ones — diminishing marginal impact on SHAP per unit of parameter change
- LogReg is flat (~0.8) across all levels — linear models are scale-invariant in this metric

**4. Stable features are low-importance features:**
- Heart: `fbs` (fasting blood sugar) is most stable — also clinically the weakest predictor in this dataset
- Diabetes: `Insulin` most stable — heavily imputed column (many original zeros)
- Breast Cancer: `mean symmetry`, `mean perimeter` most stable — secondary morphological features

## Notes

- Model-space Lipschitz uses feature_importances_ as θ proxy for XGBoost (true weights are inaccessible in tree ensembles). LogReg uses coef_ directly. This asymmetry in θ-space should be acknowledged in the paper.
- Per-feature correlation analysis shows that the most important features often have high sample-level correlation (r > 0.8) under Gaussian/dropout but collapse under label flip — confirming that label noise is uniquely destructive.
- The "most unstable = most important" pattern suggests a fragility–utility trade-off: the features most useful for prediction are the ones whose explanations are least trustworthy under data quality issues.
